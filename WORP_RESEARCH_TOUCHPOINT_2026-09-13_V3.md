## 1. Research posture remains neutral

The project is **not trying to prove that waiver/dynamic replacement is optimal**.

The original human intuition — conceptually something like `WR1* + WR1* + dynamic replacement` versus deeper fully rostered mid-tier depth — remains a hypothesis to test, not a target conclusion.

Valid outcomes still include:

- zero dynamic slots;
- one dynamic slot;
- multiple dynamic slots;
- fully rostered depth winning;
- format-dependent answers.

Do not force waiver into the result.

---

## 2. New canonical distinction: two capture problems

The research now explicitly separates:

### Market Capture
Can a FREE player be identified/acquired before his useful production?

### Lineup Capture
Once useful players are already rostered, can the manager select the correct legal starters before the week?

This creates three different value layers:

- **WoRP produced by the roster**;
- **WoRP legally capturable by the best lineup with hindsight (ORACLE)**;
- **WoRP actually/ex-ante captured in the lineup**.

Do not collapse these layers.

---

## 3. LINEUP CAPTURE AUDIT V0.3 — validated

The final validated REAL-vs-ORACLE audit used the actual 2023–2025 Sleeper format:

`QB, RB, RB, WR, WR, TE, FLEX, FLEX, FLEX, FLEX, SUPER_FLEX`

Unit: `roster × week`.

Definitions:

- **REAL** = actual Sleeper starters.
- **ORACLE** = highest realized weekly WoRP legal lineup from players already rostered that week.

Important V0.3 correction:

- every rostered QB/RB/WR/TE remains in the oracle candidate pool;
- if a rostered skill player has no row in the weekly WoRP file, position is recovered from Sleeper and weekly WoRP is set to `0.0`;
- `ORACLE < REAL` is a hard methodological failure, not a tolerable residual.

### Validation

- 648 roster-weeks;
- starter-array mismatches: **0 / 648**;
- ORACLE below REAL: **0 / 648**;
- unresolved rostered position: **0 / 648**;
- REAL starter unknown position: **2 / 648**;
- soft validation issue rate: **0.31%**;
- **PASS**.

### Main result

- REAL WoRP total: **572.518704**
- ORACLE WoRP total: **803.640290**
- REAL→ORACLE gap: **231.121586**
- mean gap / roster-week: **0.356669**
- median gap / roster-week: **0.314389**
- 100% of roster-weeks had a positive gap.

By season:

- 2023 gap: **73.848375**
- 2024 gap: **74.875959**
- 2025 gap: **82.397252**

Interpretation guardrail:

This is a **hindsight upper-bound gap**. It is not manager skill and not an ex-ante achievable capture rate.

## 4. LINEUP CAPTURE DIAGNOSTIC V0.4.1 — preliminary congestion test

The first diagnostic asked whether larger/similar player clusters were associated with a larger REAL→ORACLE lineup gap.

Important limitation:

V0.4.1 did **not** reconstruct the full bench for congestion. Its candidate universe was the union of REAL starters and ORACLE-selected players. Therefore congestion results were treated as preliminary only.

### Preliminary WR congestion result

No meaningful monotonic relationship appeared between WR similarity and lineup gap.

WR congestion quartiles by players within 0.05 prior WoRP/game of the best WR candidate:

- Q1 mean gap: **0.376207**
- Q2: **0.324343**
- Q3: **0.342015**
- Q4: **0.362239**

Continuous Spearman diagnostics:

- gap vs WR similar within 0.02: **−0.055277**
- gap vs WR similar within 0.05: **−0.077553**
- gap vs WR prior spread: **+0.030068**
- gap vs mean prior starter strength: **+0.030455**

Thus the simple story:

> “more similar WRs competing for slots → more lineup loss”

was **not supported in the preliminary diagnostic**.

Do not over-read candidate-count correlations from V0.4.1 because the REAL∪ORACLE candidate construction makes them partly endogenous to lineup divergence.

---

## 5. Slot-level decomposition warning

V0.4.1 produced apparent slot-type contributions to the REAL→ORACLE gap, including a negative SUPER_FLEX slot gap.

That is not a contradiction.

The ORACLE can move players across eligible slots, so slot-index-by-slot-index differences are not causal decompositions of positional lineup error.

Canonical rule:

> Use the **total lineup gap** as valid. Do not interpret slot-index gap as causal positional attribution.

## 6. FULL-ROSTER / BLOCKED-WORP AUDIT V0.5 — validated

V0.5 reconstructed the **entire roster**, not only REAL∪ORACLE candidates.

It separates:

### Produced Positive WoRP
Positive realized WoRP generated by all owned players.

### Missed Lineup Positive WoRP
Positive WoRP from players who were BENCHED in REAL but selected by hindsight ORACLE.

### Structurally Blocked Positive WoRP
Positive WoRP from owned players not selected even by hindsight ORACLE — i.e. value the roster produced that literally could not fit into the best legal lineup that week.

### Validation

- starter-array mismatches: **0 / 648**;
- ORACLE below REAL: **0 / 648**;
- unknown REAL starter: **2 / 648**;
- unresolved rostered position: **0 / 648**;
- positive-WoRP identity reconciliation residual: **0.000000000000**;
- soft validation issue rate: **0.31%**;
- **PASS**.

### Overall result

- owned positive WoRP: **859.741701**
- ORACLE-selected positive WoRP: **808.783672**
- missed-lineup positive WoRP: **175.801361**
- structurally blocked positive WoRP: **50.958029**
- blocked share of owned positive WoRP: **5.927132%**
- oracle-selected share of owned positive WoRP: **94.072868%**

This is a major conceptual result:

> Most positive WoRP produced by rosters **can fit legally into the optimal lineup**. The larger problem is not that value is structurally unstartable; it is that useful weekly production is difficult to select before the week.

## 9. Most important asymmetry discovered

For WR specifically:

- structurally blocked positive WoRP: **8.492443**
- missed-lineup positive WoRP: **50.826178**

Thus the historical WR problem is much larger as a **selection problem** than as a structural-capacity problem.

The same conceptual distinction applies across positions:

> **Structural blockage** = value cannot fit even with hindsight.

> **Lineup capture difficulty** = value could fit, but was not selected in REAL before the outcome was known.

This is now a central WoRP Lab distinction.

## 10. Bowers / Lamar+Josh intuition — refined

The earlier conceptual examples remain useful but must be interpreted through actual slot eligibility.

### Elite TE / Bowers-type example

A backup TE is not automatically blocked because this league has four FLEX slots that allow TE.

Therefore same-position redundancy must be measured through the actual league's full eligibility graph, not by positional label alone.

### Multiple elite QBs

QB shows the strongest structural blockage in this sample despite SUPER_FLEX eligibility.

This gives more support to the idea that excess QB depth can create true blocked roster value.

## 11. What has NOT been established

Do not claim any of the following yet:

- `WR1* + WR1* + waiver` is optimal;
- deep WR rosters are structurally inefficient;
- similar WRs necessarily create lineup ambiguity;
- REAL→ORACLE gap is manager skill;
- ORACLE gap is ex-ante capturable;
- blocked positive WoRP measures asset-price waste;
- stronger/elite starters automatically reduce lineup-capture loss;
- any fixed WR rank is a waiver/fungibility threshold.

## 12. Current research frontier

The dominant open question is no longer structural blockage.

It is:

> **How much of the ORACLE advantage is actually predictable/selectable before kickoff using only information available at the time?**

The next major audit should introduce an EX-ANTE lineup selector.

Canonical three-layer comparison:

- **REAL** — manager's actual lineup;
- **EX-ANTE** — lineup chosen using only pre-week information;
- **ORACLE** — best legal lineup after knowing weekly outcomes.

The key quantity becomes:

`ORACLE − EX-ANTE = intrinsic/model lineup-selection difficulty`

Then test whether that difficulty changes with:

- roster concentration vs depth;
- positional congestion;
- prior performance separation;
- persistent elite/auto-start players;
- repeated week-to-week superiority;
- actual slot eligibility.

This is the correct route to the original intuition:

> Is the WoRP of concentrated persistent starters easier to capture than the aggregate WoRP of several interchangeable-looking mid-tier options?
